## Longer narrative (paste)

Western New York is a national test bed for lake-effect snow, ice, and increasingly violent warm-season storms. The National Weather Service and Storm Prediction Center already publish the data. Last-mile and warehouse workers in this corridor often learn about a threat from a phone alert or a canceled school day — not from an employer briefing timed to a watch/warning.

I am an independent weather journalist (Doc Weather) covering WNY and national outlooks with NWS/SPC source data, and I spent August 2026 off work with a broken ankle after a period of warehouse/last-mile employment. I am not asking FIJ to fund my medical bills. I am asking for seed money to find out, with records, whether the injury-and-attendance regime in this industry ignores forecasts the public already paid for.

**Why I think there is a bigger story**
- Public NWS/SPC products are free and timestamped. Employer comms can be FOILed only for public agencies; private employers require worker testimony, NLRB/OSHA dockets, and state labor filings.
- Lake-effect and lake-enhanced events are locally extreme while remaining “just weather” in national coverage.
- OSHA and workers’ comp data for warehousing/last-mile in Erie and Monroe counties are underused in local TV weather.

**Preliminary reporting this seed would buy**
1. FOIL/records: NY DOL, OSHA establishment search, workers’ comp board public statistical files, municipal emergency-management after-action reports for 2024–2026 lake-effect and heat events.
2. Source map: 8–12 current/former WNY warehouse and DSP drivers (first names only in the seed memo).
3. Product audit: for 5 named event days, compare NWS watch/warning issuance times vs publicly visible Amazon Flex / DSP group-chat / municipal school-close times.
4. Clip search: Buffalo News, D&C, WGRZ, Spectrum — confirm what has *not* been investigated.

If the records show a pattern (warnings issued, shifts continuing, injuries clustering), I return to FIJ for a regular grant with an outlet letter. If they do not, the seed still produces a public methods note: how to overlay NWS timestamps on labor incidents.
